4. Safety, wellbeing and responsible use

Build the awareness, judgement and practical skills needed to use digital technologies safely, responsibly and sustainably while protecting information, supporting wellbeing and reducing harm.

Official DigComp 3.0 area description

To protect devices, content, personal data and privacy in digital environments. To support physical, mental and social wellbeing of oneself and others, and to be aware of the benefits and risks of digital technologies for wellbeing and social inclusion. To be aware of the environmental impact of digital technologies and their use, to take action to reduce such impact, and to use digital technologies to support sustainability.

Why this area matters

Queensland public sector employees use digital technologies every day to access information, deliver services, collaborate with colleagues and engage with stakeholders.

Strong capability in this area helps employees protect information, manage cyber risks, maintain wellbeing, use technology responsibly and contribute to sustainable digital practices.

Using workplace tools

Tool availability and approval differ between agencies.

Use only systems approved by your agency and follow applicable information security, privacy, records management, cyber security and AI requirements.

4.1 Protecting devices

What it is

Applying safety and cyber security measures to protect devices, information and digital environments.

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Government information and systems are valuable assets.

Employees play a critical role in protecting devices, information and services from cyber threats, unauthorised access and accidental loss.

Workplace scenarios

  • Locking devices when unattended
  • Applying software updates
  • Recognising phishing emails
  • Reporting cyber security incidents
  • Managing passwords and authentication
  • Working securely from different locations

Tools and processes

  • Multi-factor authentication
  • Microsoft Defender
  • Agency cyber security controls
  • Password managers where approved
  • Security awareness training
  • Device management tools

Try it at work

Review:

  • Password practices
  • MFA settings
  • Software updates
  • Device lock settings
  • Reporting processes

Hint: If something looks suspicious, report it through the approved process rather than investigating independently.

4.2 Protecting personal data and privacy

What it is

Understanding, protecting and responsibly managing personal information and privacy in digital environments.

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Employees often handle personal and sensitive information.

Strong privacy practices support trust, legal compliance and responsible service delivery.

Workplace scenarios

  • Handling customer information
  • Managing permissions
  • Sharing information appropriately
  • Responding to privacy concerns
  • Using AI tools responsibly

Tools and processes

  • Privacy impact assessments
  • Information classification
  • Permission controls
  • Queensland privacy requirements
  • Records management practices

Try it at work

Review one current process involving personal information and identify:

  • what information is collected
  • who can access it
  • whether access is justified
  • how information is protected

Hint: Only collect, access or share personal information when there is a legitimate business need.

4.3 Supporting wellbeing

What it is

Using digital technologies in ways that support wellbeing, inclusion and healthy workplace practices.

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Technology provides significant benefits but can also contribute to stress, fatigue, distraction and exclusion.

Employees should understand how to use digital technologies in ways that support their own wellbeing and that of others.

Workplace scenarios

  • Managing notifications
  • Hybrid working
  • Online meetings
  • Digital workload management
  • Supporting inclusion and accessibility

Tools and processes

  • Teams status settings
  • Focus time
  • Accessibility features
  • Workplace wellbeing programs
  • Inclusive communication practices

Try it at work

Review your digital workday and identify:

  • notification overload
  • unnecessary interruptions
  • opportunities for focus time
  • opportunities to improve accessibility

Hint: Not every communication requires an immediate response.

4.4 Environmental impacts of digital technologies

What it is

Understanding how digital technologies affect the environment and identifying opportunities to reduce impacts.

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Digital technologies rely on energy, infrastructure and physical resources.

Employees can contribute to sustainability through responsible technology use and informed decision-making.

Workplace scenarios

  • Managing device lifecycles
  • Reducing unnecessary storage
  • Choosing sustainable practices
  • Supporting paper reduction initiatives
  • Using digital tools efficiently

Tools and processes

  • Device lifecycle management
  • Sustainable procurement approaches
  • Cloud storage management
  • Digital recordkeeping systems

Try it at work

Review:

  • duplicate files
  • unnecessary storage
  • unused information
  • outdated content

and identify opportunities for responsible management.

Hint: Sustainability is not only about devices. It also includes the information and services we keep and maintain.
