1. Information search, evaluation and management

Build the judgement and practical skills needed to find, assess, organise and use digital information effectively in Queensland Government work.

Official DigComp 3.0 area description

To articulate information needs, and to search for, locate and retrieve digital information and content. To judge the relevance of the source and its content in digital environments. To critically evaluate digital sources, content, and processes used to generate them. To store, manage, organise and analyse digital information and data.

Why this area matters

Queensland public sector employees routinely use digital information to prepare advice, design services, administer programs, manage projects and make decisions.

Strong capability in this area helps employees find authoritative information, recognise unreliable or AI-generated content, and manage government information so it remains secure, discoverable and useful.

Using workplace tools

Tool availability and approval differ between agencies.

Use only systems approved by your agency and follow applicable information security, privacy, recordkeeping and AI requirements.

1.1 Browsing, searching and filtering information

What it is

Articulating an information need, choosing where and how to search, refining a query and distinguishing relevant from irrelevant results.

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Employees need to locate current policies, legislation, procedures, research, data and operational guidance efficiently.

Better search practices reduce duplicated effort and help ensure advice is based on the right source and version.

Workplace scenarios

  • Finding the current ForGov policy or whole-of-government standard
  • Locating the latest approved brief, template or project record in SharePoint
  • Searching legislation, open data, research or agency intranet content

Tools and processes

  • Microsoft Search
  • SharePoint and Teams search
  • ForGov
  • Queensland Legislation
  • data.qld.gov.au
  • OneNote tags and search
  • Copilot or approved AI-assisted search with source checking

Try it at work

  1. Write one sentence describing the information needed for a current brief or task.
  2. Choose the most authoritative source likely to hold it.
  3. Run two searches using different keywords or phrases.
  4. Apply a date, source or document-type filter.
  5. Compare the results for relevance, currency and authority.
  6. Record the final source in the work notes or brief.

Hint: If an AI-assisted search provides a summary, open and check the original source before relying on any important claim.

Official DigComp 3.0 proficiency statements

Basic

Recall and perform simple search tasks with guidance, using basic queries and familiar sources.

Intermediate

Independently define search needs, select appropriate tools, refine queries and apply filters across common sources.

Advanced

Design and combine search strategies across multiple sources, evaluate results for relevance and accuracy, and advise colleagues.

Highly advanced

Lead improvements to search approaches, design specialised retrieval methods and develop standards for complex information discovery.

Suggested next practice: Repeat the search activity using a different source, then compare which search terms, filters and tools produced the most relevant result. Save the effective search pattern in OneNote for later use.

1.2 Evaluating information

What it is

Assessing and comparing the credibility and reliability of digital sources and content, including the processes used to generate them.

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Public sector advice and decisions need evidence that is relevant, current and trustworthy.

Employees must recognise misinformation, bias, unsupported claims and plausible but inaccurate AI output, then confirm significant information against authoritative sources.

Workplace scenarios

  • Checking evidence used in a policy brief or ministerial response
  • Comparing research findings from government, academic and commercial sources
  • Validating statistics, quotations or summaries produced with Copilot

Tools and processes

  • Source, date, author and purpose checks
  • Cross-checking against original reports and datasets
  • Excel or OneNote evidence logs
  • Approved Copilot tools with human review

Try it at work

Five-point check:

  1. Who produced it?
  2. What evidence supports it?
  3. How current is it?
  4. What interests, assumptions or biases may influence it?
  5. Can it be confirmed using an independent authoritative source?

Hint: Keep the original source link with the final claim so another person can verify it quickly.

Official DigComp 3.0 proficiency statements

Basic

Recognise simple indicatorsof source reliability and make straightforward credibility checks with guidance.

Intermediate

Independently assess authoritativeness, detect common bias or AI limitations, and cross-check claims against primary sources.

Advanced

Systematically evaluate diverse evidence, explain limitations and uncertainties, and support others in rigorous source appraisal.

Highly advanced

Lead evidence appraisal practices, design evaluation frameworks and institutional approaches that strengthen information integrity.

Suggested next practice: Create a reusable source-check template in OneNote with six prompts: authority, currency, relevance, accuracy, purpose and government context. Apply it to one source each week.

1.3 Managing information

What it is

Organising, storing and retrieving information and data, and collecting, processing and analysing information in structured digital environments.

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Government information is both a working asset and evidence of business activity.

Good management supports accountability, continuity, lawful recordkeeping, efficient collaboration and the ability to find and reuse reliable information.

Workplace scenarios

  • Structuring a SharePoint library for a cross-agency project
  • Applying file names, metadata, permissions and version control
  • Organising meeting notes in OneNote and capturing final decisions in approved recordkeeping systems

Tools and processes

  • SharePoint
  • OneDrive
  • Teams
  • OneNote
  • Outlook
  • Approved records systems
  • Excel tables
  • Power BI

Try it at work

  1. Write one sentence describing the information needed for a current brief or task.
  2. Choose the most authoritative source likely to hold it.
  3. Run two searches using different keywords or phrases.
  4. Apply a date, source or document-type filter.
  5. Compare the results for relevance, currency and authority.
  6. Record the final source in the work notes or brief.

Hint: Do not delete government information unless authorised under applicable recordkeeping requirements.

Official DigComp 3.0 proficiency statements

Basic

Perform straightforward information management tasks (saving, retrieving and organising) with guidance.

Intermediate

Apply naming conventions, metadata and basic versioning; manage cloud files and permissions independently.

Advanced

Design organised information structures, apply governance practices and support others to maintain discoverable records.

Highly advanced

Lead information governance initiatives, design scalable recordkeeping approaches and improve institutional information practices.

Suggested next practice: Agree a simple naming and metadata convention with your team, test it on one active workspace for two weeks, and ask a colleague whether information is easier to find.
