5. Problem identification and solving

Build the practical skills needed to identify digital needs, solve problems, improve processes, adapt technologies and continue developing capability in a rapidly changing digital environment.

Official DigComp 3.0 area description

To identify and assess needs, and to use digital technologies and adapt digital environments to meet these needs. To identify and resolve technical and conceptual problems and problem situations in digital environments. To use digital technologies to make improvements in, or new solutions for, processes and products. To build capabilities to operate autonomously in digital environments. To stay informed about digital technological developments and their implications.

Why this area matters

Queensland Government employees regularly encounter technical issues, process challenges, changing technologies and opportunities to improve services.

Strong capability in this area helps employees solve problems efficiently, make better use of digital tools, adapt to change, improve service delivery and continue building capability over time.

Using workplace tools

Tool availability and approval differ between agencies.

Employees should use approved technologies and follow applicable agency requirements, information management obligations, cyber security requirements and technology governance processes.

5.1 Identifying and solving technical problems

What it is

Recognising technical issues and using appropriate processes and tools to resolve them.

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Small technical issues can significantly disrupt productivity, collaboration and service delivery.

Employees who can diagnose problems and seek appropriate support reduce disruption and improve efficiency.

Workplace scenarios

  • Printer not working
  • Access problems
  • Software issues
  • Login failures
  • Teams meeting problems
  • Connectivity issues
  • SharePoint access issues

Tools and processes

  • Service desk
  • Microsoft Teams support features
  • ServiceNow or equivalent agency platform
  • Knowledge bases
  • User guides
  • Device troubleshooting tools

Try it at work

The next time a technical issue occurs:

  • identify the problem
  • record the symptoms
  • review available guidance
  • test possible solutions
  • escalate appropriately if required

Hint: Focus on the problem symptoms first before assuming the cause.

How capability develops

Basic

Recognises common technical issues and follows simple troubleshooting steps with support.

Intermediate

Identifies likely causes and resolves common technical problems independently.

Advanced

Resolves more complex technical issues and supports others to troubleshoot effectively.

Highly advanced

Leads problem-solving in specialised environments and contributes to improvements in technical support processes.

Official DigComp 3.0 proficiency statements

Basic

Competence statement: To identify technical problems when operating devices and using digital environments, and to solve them (from trouble-shooting to solving more complex problems).

DigComp proficiency levels: Level 1: with guidance. Level 2: with autonomy and with appropriate guidance when needed.

Intermediate

Competence statement: To identify technical problems when operating devices and using digital environments, and to solve them (from trouble-shooting to solving more complex problems).

DigComp proficiency levels: Level 3: on my own and solving straightforward problems. Level 4: independently, according to my needs, and solving well-defined non-routine problems.

Advanced

Competence statement: To identify technical problems when operating devices and using digital environments, and to solve them (from trouble-shooting to solving more complex problems).

DigComp proficiency levels: Level 5: as well as guiding others. Level 6: adapting to others in complex contexts.

Highly advanced

Competence statement: To identify technical problems when operating devices and using digital environments, and to solve them (from trouble-shooting to solving more complex problems).

DigComp proficiency levels: Level 7: in complex contexts with limited solutions. Level 8: in complex contexts with many interacting factors.

Suggested next practice: Review one recurring technical issue in your team and document the most effective resolution steps.

5.2 Identifying needs and digital technological responses

What it is

Assessing needs and selecting, adapting or using technologies that best meet those needs.

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Not every problem requires a new technology solution.

Employees need to assess needs objectively and determine whether existing tools can be used more effectively before introducing something new.

Workplace scenarios

  • Choosing between Teams and email
  • Selecting collaboration tools
  • Designing digital forms
  • Improving workflows
  • Accessibility adjustments
  • Supporting diverse user needs

Tools and processes

  • Microsoft 365 tools
  • Accessibility settings
  • Forms
  • Power Platform
  • Process mapping
  • User-centred design approaches

Try it at work

Review a current process and ask:

  • what is the actual need?
  • who is affected?
  • what technologies already exist?
  • what barriers exist?
  • what would improve the experience?

Hint: Start with the user need rather than the technology.

How capability develops

Basic

Uses familiar technologies to meet simple needs.

Intermediate

Selects appropriate technologies and adapts settings to suit circumstances.

Advanced

Evaluates alternative approaches and supports others to improve technology use.

Highly advanced

Designs and improves technology responses to complex organisational needs.

Official DigComp 3.0 proficiency statements

Basic

Competence statement: To assess needs and to identify, evaluate, select and use digital tools and possible technological responses and to solve them. To adjust and customise digital environments to personal needs (e.g. accessibility).

DigComp proficiency levels: Level 1: with guidance. Level 2: with autonomy and with appropriate guidance when needed.

Intermediate

Competence statement: To assess needs and to identify, evaluate, select and use digital tools and possible technological responses and to solve them. To adjust and customise digital environments to personal needs (e.g. accessibility).

DigComp proficiency levels: Level 3: on my own and solving straightforward problems. Level 4: independently, according to my needs, and solving well-defined non-routine problems.

Advanced

Competence statement: To assess needs and to identify, evaluate, select and use digital tools and possible technological responses and to solve them. To adjust and customise digital environments to personal needs (e.g. accessibility).

DigComp proficiency levels: Level 5: as well as guiding others. Level 6: adapting to others in complex contexts.

Highly advanced

Competence statement: To assess needs and to identify, evaluate, select and use digital tools and possible technological responses and to solve them. To adjust and customise digital environments to personal needs (e.g. accessibility).

DigComp proficiency levels: Level 7: in complex contexts with limited solutions. Level 8: in complex contexts with many interacting factors.

Suggested next practice: Identify one manual process and evaluate whether existing approved tools could improve it.

5.3 Identifying creative solutions using digital technologies

What it is

Using digital technologies creatively to improve processes, services and outcomes.

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Innovation often happens through small improvements.

Employees who can identify opportunities and use technology creatively contribute to more effective, efficient and responsive services.

Workplace scenarios

  • Automating repetitive tasks
  • Improving reporting
  • Creating dashboards
  • Streamlining approvals
  • Designing service improvements
  • Improving customer experiences

Tools and processes

  • Power Automate
  • Power Apps
  • Power BI
  • Excel
  • Forms
  • Workflow mapping
  • Process improvement techniques

Try it at work

Identify:

  • one repetitive task
  • one bottleneck
  • one manual process

and explore ways to simplify or improve it using approved technologies.

Hint: A simple improvement can often provide greater value than a complex solution.

How capability develops

Basic

Recognises opportunities where technology may help solve problems.

Intermediate

Uses existing digital tools to improve tasks and processes.

Advanced

Develops and implements new approaches using digital technologies.

Highly advanced

Leads innovation and contributes to new digital solutions for complex challenges.

Intermediate

5.4 Identifying and addressing digital capability needs

What it is

Recognising digital capability gaps and taking action to build knowledge, skills and confidence.

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Digital technologies continue to evolve.

Employees need to understand their own capability needs and identify opportunities for continuous improvement.

Workplace scenarios

  • Learning a new system
  • Building confidence with data tools
  • Developing AI literacy
  • Improving collaboration capability
  • Participating in professional development

Tools and processes

  • Digital capability assessments
  • Learning pathways
  • APS Academy
  • Microsoft Learn
  • Agency training programs
  • Professional communities of practice

Try it at work

Identify one capability area you would like to strengthen in the next three months and create a simple learning plan.

Hint: Capability development is most effective when linked to real workplace tasks and goals.

How capability develops

Basic

Recognises that capability development is important and seeks guidance when needed.

Intermediate

Actively develops capability and applies new learning independently.

Advanced

Supports others to build digital capability and shares knowledge.

Highly advanced

Leads capability-building initiatives and contributes to workforce development approaches.
