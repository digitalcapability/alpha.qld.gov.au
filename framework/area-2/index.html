2. Communication and collaboration

Communication and collaboration capability helps Queensland Government employees interact, share knowledge, work effectively with others, participate in digital environments, support inclusive communication and manage their digital presence and reputation.

Official DigComp 3.0 area description

To interact, share, communicate and collaborate in digital environments while being aware of cultural, generational and other diversity and the features and limitations of digital technologies. To participate in society through digital technologies. To assert one's rights and exercise choice in digital environments. To manage one’s digital presence, identity and reputation.

Why this area matters

Queensland Government work depends on communication, collaboration and engagement across teams, agencies, stakeholders and communities.

Employees regularly communicate using Teams, Outlook, SharePoint, online collaboration platforms and digital services. Strong capability in this area helps people communicate appropriately, collaborate efficiently, participate safely in digital environments and manage their digital identity professionally.

Using workplace tools

Tool availability and approval varies across agencies.

Employees should only use approved communication, collaboration and AI-enabled tools in accordance with agency requirements, information security obligations, privacy obligations and records management requirements.

2.1 Interacting through digital technologies

What it is

Using digital technologies to communicate, engage and interact with others appropriately, effectively and purposefully. This includes selecting suitable digital communication channels, participating in digital environments and adapting communication approaches to different audiences and contexts.

In practical terms, this means being able to answer:

"What is the most effective way to communicate and engage with others digitally?"

rather than simply:

"What tool should I use?"

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Queensland Government employees communicate and collaborate with colleagues, stakeholders, customers and communities using a wide range of digital technologies.

Effective digital interaction supports:

  • Better collaboration and teamwork
  • Improved service delivery
  • Faster information sharing
  • More inclusive participation
  • Better stakeholder engagement
  • Improved customer experiences
  • Productive remote and hybrid working

Choosing the right communication method can improve clarity, reduce misunderstandings and ensure information reaches the right people at the right time.

Digital interaction also requires employees to consider accessibility, inclusivity, professionalism and the needs of different audiences.

Workplace scenarios

You may be demonstrating this competency when you:

  • Select the most appropriate channel to communicate a sensitive issue, balancing urgency, audience needs and recordkeeping requirements.
  • Facilitate a hybrid meeting where in-person and remote participants can contribute equally.
  • Adapt digital communications to support accessibility, including plain language and inclusive presentation formats.
Tools and processes

Tool availability and configuration differ between agencies. Use only systems approved by your agency.

Common tools

Tools are the digital technologies used to communicate and interact with others.

Examples include:

  • Microsoft Teams
  • Outlook
  • SharePoint
  • OneDrive sharing features
  • Microsoft Forms
  • Viva Engage (where enabled)
  • Video conferencing platforms
  • Intranets and collaboration platforms
  • Customer and stakeholder engagement platforms
  • Approved messaging and communication systems

Common processes

Processes are the ways of working that help communication remain clear, effective, accessible and professional.

Examples include:

  • Selecting appropriate communication channels
  • Following communication protocols and standards
  • Using meeting and collaboration tools effectively
  • Sharing information using agreed processes
  • Practising respectful and professional communication
  • Considering accessibility requirements
  • Managing communication preferences and expectations
  • Maintaining appropriate records of key decisions and actions
  • Engaging stakeholders through suitable digital channels
  • Using digital tools to support collaboration and participation
Apply this competency at work
  1. Think about a recent piece of communication or collaboration activity.
  2. Identify the digital channel that was used.
  3. Consider whether it was the best channel for the purpose.
  4. Identify who the audience was.
  5. Consider whether accessibility or inclusion needs were taken into account.
  6. Reflect on whether the message achieved its intended outcome.
  7. Identify one way the communication approach could be improved.
  8. Apply that improvement to your next digital interaction.

Hint: Effective digital communication is not about using the newest tool. It is about choosing the most appropriate tool and approach for the audience, purpose and context.

Digital Communication Planner

Objective

Plan and deliver a digital interaction using the channel and approach that best fit the audience, purpose and context.

Learning outcomes

After completing this activity, you will be able to:

  • Define a communication objective.
  • Choose an appropriate digital channel.
  • Adapt communication for audience and accessibility needs.
  • Review whether the interaction achieved its purpose.

Instructions

Using the downloadable template:

  1. Choose a current communication activity.
  2. Write the communication objective in one sentence.
  3. Identify the audience and what the audience needs to know or do.
  4. List available channels and compare their strengths and limitations.
  5. Choose a channel and explain the choice.
  6. Plan the message, timing, accessibility and follow-up.

Reflection

  • Was the message clear and inclusive?
  • What would you change next time?

Evidence of completion

  • Options are compared and a channel is selected.

2.2 Sharing through digital technologies

What it is

Using digital technologies to share information, data and digital content with others appropriately, securely and effectively. This includes selecting suitable sharing methods, managing access to information, understanding permissions and ensuring information is shared responsibly.

In practical terms, this means being able to answer:

"How can I share the right information with the right people in the right way?"

rather than simply:

"How do I send this file?"

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Queensland Government employees routinely share information with colleagues, stakeholders, agencies, suppliers and customers.

Effective digital sharing helps:

  • Improve collaboration and teamwork
  • Reduce duplication of effort
  • Ensure people have access to the information they need
  • Support consistent and informed decision-making
  • Improve productivity and service delivery
  • Maintain organisational knowledge

Information must also be shared responsibly.

Employees need to consider:

  • Privacy obligations
  • Information security requirements
  • Access permissions
  • Recordkeeping requirements
  • Intellectual property considerations
  • Agency policies and procedures

Sharing information effectively is about balancing collaboration with appropriate controls.

Workplace scenarios

You may be demonstrating this competency when you:

  • Share project information with internal and external stakeholders while ensuring permissions and information classifications are applied correctly.
  • Publish guidance materials through approved platforms so staff can access current and accurate information.
  • Coordinate cross-agency information sharing while maintaining privacy, confidentiality and governance requirements.
Tools and processes

Tool availability and configuration differ between agencies. Use only systems approved by your agency.

Common tools

Tools are the digital technologies used to share information, content and data with others.

Examples include:

  • SharePoint
  • Teams
  • OneDrive
  • Outlook
  • Power BI sharing and distribution features
  • Intranets
  • Content management systems
  • Knowledge management platforms
  • Approved file-sharing systems
  • Approved collaboration platforms

Common processes

Processes are the agreed ways of working that help information be shared securely, appropriately and efficiently.

Examples include:

  • Applying access permissions
  • Managing sharing settings
  • Following information security requirements
  • Following privacy obligations
  • Applying recordkeeping requirements
  • Determining information ownership
  • Reviewing information before sharing
  • Using links rather than duplicate attachments where appropriate
  • Controlling access to sensitive information
  • Maintaining version control when collaborating
Apply this competency at work
  1. Select a document, report or resource that you regularly share.
  2. Review how it is currently shared.
  3. Identify who genuinely needs access.
  4. Check whether permissions are appropriate.
  5. Consider whether a link could be used instead of sending attachments.
  6. Confirm that the information is current and approved for sharing.
  7. Test whether recipients can access the information successfully.
  8. Identify one improvement that would make sharing more secure or efficient.

Hint: Effective sharing is not about giving everyone access to everything. It is about ensuring the right people can access the right information at the right time.

2.3 Participating in society through digital technologies

What it is

Using digital technologies to participate in civic, community, professional and organisational activities. This includes accessing digital services, engaging with communities, contributing to public discussions, participating in digital decision-making processes and using digital technologies to support active citizenship and professional participation.

In practical terms, this means being able to answer:

"How can I use digital technologies to contribute, participate and create positive outcomes?"

rather than simply:

"How do I access this service?"

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Queensland Government employees are expected to participate effectively in digital environments, both as public servants and as contributors to organisational and professional communities.

Digital technologies increasingly shape how governments engage with communities, consult stakeholders, deliver services and make decisions.

Strong participation skills help employees:

  • Contribute to communities of practice and professional networks
  • Participate in digital consultation and engagement activities
  • Engage with stakeholders through digital channels
  • Support digital service delivery
  • Share knowledge and lessons learned
  • Collaborate across agencies and jurisdictions
  • Promote inclusive participation and engagement
  • Better understand community needs and expectations

Digital participation is becoming increasingly important as more services, consultations, communities and professional networks operate online.

Workplace scenarios

You may be demonstrating this competency when you:

  • Use digital consultation platforms to gather community feedback and ensure responses are captured transparently.
  • Help colleagues understand how to access and use government digital services safely and effectively.
  • Contribute to online engagement activities that encourage informed participation in government programs or initiatives.
Tools and processes

Tool availability and configuration differ between agencies. Use only systems approved by your agency.

Common tools

Tools are the digital technologies used to participate, contribute and engage with others.

Examples include:

  • Microsoft Teams
  • SharePoint
  • Viva Engage (where enabled)
  • Microsoft Forms
  • Webinars and virtual event platforms
  • Online consultation platforms
  • Professional networking platforms
  • Government service portals
  • Community and stakeholder engagement platforms
  • Communities of practice and knowledge-sharing platforms

Common processes

Processes are the ways of working that help participation remain inclusive, respectful, effective and purposeful.

Examples include:

  • Participating in professional communities
  • Contributing to collaborative activities
  • Sharing knowledge and expertise
  • Providing constructive feedback
  • Supporting digital consultation activities
  • Following expectations for professional conduct
  • Engaging respectfully with diverse viewpoints
  • Supporting accessibility and inclusion
  • Participating in organisational improvement activities
  • Using digital channels to support community engagement
Apply this competency at work
  1. Identify a digital community, network or collaboration space you are a member of.
  2. Reflect on how often you actively contribute.
  3. Review recent discussions, activities or opportunities to participate.
  4. Share a useful resource, lesson learned or insight relevant to the group.
  5. Respond constructively to at least one other contribution.
  6. Identify how participation could benefit your work, team or stakeholders.
  7. Reflect on the outcome of your contribution.
  8. Identify one way to increase your participation over the next month.

Hint: Meaningful participation is not about being the most active contributor. It is about providing useful, respectful and relevant contributions that add value for others.

2.4 Collaborating through digital technologies

What it is

Using digital technologies to work together towards shared goals. This includes co-creating content, coordinating activities, sharing responsibilities, managing workflows and contributing to collective outcomes through digital environments.

In practical terms, this means being able to answer:

"How can we work together effectively using digital technologies?"

rather than simply:

"How can I work on my part?"

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Queensland Government work is increasingly delivered through cross-functional teams, projects, programs, communities of practice and partnerships.

Effective digital collaboration helps employees:

  • Work productively across locations and agencies
  • Reduce duplication and rework
  • Coordinate activities and responsibilities
  • Share knowledge and expertise
  • Improve decision-making
  • Deliver services and projects more effectively
  • Support hybrid and flexible work arrangements
  • Create better outcomes for customers and stakeholders

Strong collaboration skills are increasingly important as more work is conducted through shared digital workspaces rather than individual documents and email chains.

Digital collaboration is most effective when people, processes and technology work together.

Workplace scenarios

You may be demonstrating this competency when you:

  • Coordinate a multi-disciplinary project team through shared workspaces, version-controlled documents and collaborative planning tools.
  • Use approved AI-enabled features to support collaboration while maintaining human oversight of decisions and outputs.
  • Establish clear roles, responsibilities and workflows within a digital project environment to improve delivery outcomes.
Tools and processes

Tool availability and configuration differ between agencies. Use only systems approved by your agency.

Common tools

Tools are the digital technologies used to collaborate and work together.

Examples include:

  • Microsoft Teams
  • SharePoint
  • OneDrive
  • Microsoft Loop
  • OneNote
  • Outlook
  • Microsoft Planner
  • Microsoft To Do
  • Microsoft Forms
  • Collaborative workspaces and project management tools

Common processes

Processes are the agreed ways of working that help collaboration remain effective, transparent and productive.

Examples include:

  • Co-authoring documents
  • Sharing responsibilities and actions
  • Providing constructive feedback
  • Managing version control
  • Using collaboration spaces effectively
  • Maintaining shared records of decisions
  • Coordinating activities across teams
  • Tracking actions and progress
  • Following agreed governance and approval processes
  • Supporting inclusive participation
Apply this competency at work
  1. Identify a piece of work that currently involves multiple people.
  2. Review how collaboration is currently occurring.
  3. Identify any reliance on emailed attachments or disconnected versions.
  4. Move the work into an appropriate shared collaboration space if approved.
  5. Use comments or collaboration tools to gather feedback.
  6. Track actions, decisions and responsibilities in a shared location.
  7. Ask collaborators what worked well and what could be improved.
  8. Identify one change that would make future collaboration more effective.

Hint: Effective collaboration is not about using more tools. It is about making it easier for people to contribute, coordinate and work together towards a shared outcome.

2.5 Digital Behaviour

What it is

Applying appropriate, respectful and responsible behaviour when communicating and interacting in digital environments. This includes understanding social norms and expectations, adapting communication for different audiences and contexts, and promoting safe, inclusive and constructive online interactions.

In practical terms, this means being able to answer:

"How can I communicate and interact professionally and respectfully in digital environments?"

rather than simply:

"Can I send this message?"

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Queensland Government employees communicate and collaborate with colleagues, leaders, stakeholders, customers and communities through a range of digital channels.

The way people behave online and in digital workspaces can influence:

  • Workplace culture
  • Collaboration and teamwork
  • Stakeholder relationships
  • Community trust and confidence
  • Inclusion and accessibility
  • Psychological safety
  • Organisational reputation

Strong digital etiquette skills help employees:

  • Communicate professionally and respectfully
  • Build positive working relationships
  • Reduce misunderstandings and conflict
  • Support inclusive participation
  • Foster productive online discussions
  • Contribute to a safe and respectful workplace

Digital communication can sometimes feel less personal than face-to-face communication, making it even more important to communicate thoughtfully and respectfully.

Workplace scenarios

You may be demonstrating this competency when you:

  • Manage a difficult online discussion respectfully and professionally while maintaining focus on outcomes.
  • Challenge inappropriate behaviour in a digital workspace and reinforce expected standards of conduct.
  • Model inclusive communication practices that encourage participation from people with diverse experiences and backgrounds.
Tools and processes

Tool availability and configuration differ between agencies. Use only systems approved by your agency.

Common tools

Tools are the digital technologies used to communicate and interact with others.

Examples include:

  • Microsoft Teams
  • Outlook
  • SharePoint comments and discussions
  • Microsoft Loop
  • OneNote collaboration spaces
  • Viva Engage (where enabled)
  • Webinar and virtual event platforms
  • Community and discussion forums
  • Customer and stakeholder engagement platforms

Common processes

Processes are the behaviours and practices that support respectful, productive and inclusive digital interactions.

Examples include:

  • Applying professional communication standards
  • Considering audience, tone and context
  • Using respectful and inclusive language
  • Providing constructive feedback
  • Following meeting etiquette
  • Supporting accessibility and inclusion
  • Respecting diverse opinions and perspectives
  • Managing conflict professionally
  • Being mindful of digital wellbeing and communication overload
  • Modelling positive behaviour in digital environments
Apply this competency at work
  1. Review a recent email, Teams message or online discussion you participated in.
  2. Consider the audience and purpose of the communication.
  3. Reflect on the tone that was used.
  4. Consider whether the message was clear, respectful and professional.
  5. Identify any assumptions or wording that could have been misunderstood.
  6. Ask yourself whether the communication encouraged constructive engagement.
  7. Identify one improvement you could make to future digital communications.
  8. Apply that improvement the next time you communicate online.

Hint: Good digital etiquette is not about being formal all the time. It is about communicating in a way that is respectful, appropriate and effective for the audience and situation.

2.6 Managing digital identity

What it is

Creating and managing one or more digital identities, protecting online reputation, and understanding how digital presence, actions and interactions contribute to how individuals and organisations are perceived in digital environments.

In practical terms, this means being able to answer:

"What does my digital presence say about me, my role and my organisation?"

rather than simply:

"What information about me exists online?"

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Queensland Government employees interact with a wide range of digital systems, online services, collaboration tools and professional networks.

Every digital interaction contributes to a person's digital identity and, in some cases, can influence the reputation of their team, agency or the Queensland Government more broadly.

Strong digital identity management helps employees:

  • Maintain professional credibility
  • Build trust with colleagues and stakeholders
  • Protect personal and organisational reputation
  • Manage information shared online
  • Use professional networks appropriately
  • Reduce risks associated with oversharing
  • Understand how digital activities create a lasting digital footprint
  • Navigate digital environments safely and responsibly

Digital identity is not limited to public social media. It also includes profiles, accounts, contributions, interactions and content created across workplace digital systems.

Workplace scenarios

You may be demonstrating this competency when you:

  • Maintain a professional digital presence that accurately reflects your role and responsibilities.
  • Review privacy and profile settings across work-related platforms to minimise unnecessary exposure of personal information.
  • Manage multiple digital identities appropriately when working across projects, systems or stakeholder groups.
Tools and processes

Tool availability and configuration differ between agencies. Use only systems approved by your agency.

Common tools

Tools are the digital technologies used to establish, maintain and manage digital identities.

Examples include:

  • Microsoft 365 profiles
  • Teams profile cards
  • Outlook profiles and signatures
  • LinkedIn
  • Professional networking platforms
  • Agency intranets
  • Communities of practice
  • Online collaboration platforms
  • Identity and access management systems
  • Social media platforms used in accordance with agency requirements

Common processes

Processes are the actions and behaviours used to manage digital identity and reputation effectively.

Examples include:

  • Maintaining accurate profile information
  • Reviewing digital footprints
  • Managing privacy and visibility settings
  • Separating personal and professional activities where appropriate
  • Monitoring professional online presence
  • Managing online reputation
  • Using professional communication standards
  • Following agency social media and communication policies
  • Managing access credentials appropriately
  • Reviewing information shared publicly
Apply this competency at work
  1. Review one of your professional digital profiles or accounts.
  2. Check that your role, contact details and profile information are accurate and current.
  3. Search for your name using an approved search engine.
  4. Review what information is publicly visible.
  5. Consider whether the information supports your professional identity.
  6. Review privacy and visibility settings where appropriate.
  7. Identify any information that is inaccurate, outdated or unnecessary.
  8. Make one improvement that strengthens your professional digital presence.

Hint: Managing digital identity is not about removing your digital presence. It is about ensuring your digital presence accurately reflects who you are and how you want to be perceived.
