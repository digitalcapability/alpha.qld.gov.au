3. Content creation

Content creation capability helps Queensland Government employees create, edit, improve and manage digital content responsibly. It supports effective communication, service delivery, knowledge sharing, innovation and problem-solving through the purposeful use of digital technologies.

Official DigComp 3.0 area description

To create and edit digital content. To improve and integrate information and content into an existing body of knowledge while understanding how copyright and licences are to be applied, adopting an ethical and responsible approach in the creation, improvement and integration of digital content. To know how to apply computational thinking and programming techniques to give instructions to a computer system.

Why this area matters

Queensland Government employees create digital content every day, including briefs, reports, procedures, presentations, web content, records, dashboards, forms, guidance material, data products and communication resources.

Strong capability in this area helps employees create clear, accessible, accurate and responsible content. It also supports better information sharing, improved service delivery and more effective use of digital tools, including approved AI-enabled tools.

Using workplace tools

Tool availability and approval varies across agencies.

Employees should only use approved content creation, collaboration, automation and AI-enabled tools in accordance with agency requirements, information security, privacy, copyright, accessibility and recordkeeping obligations.

3.1 Developing digital content

What it is

Creating and editing digital content in different formats. This includes developing documents, presentations, spreadsheets, forms, images, videos and other digital resources, as well as improving and adapting existing content for different audiences and purposes.

In practical terms, this means being able to answer:

"How can I create digital content that is useful, clear and fit for purpose?"

rather than simply:

"How do I make this document?"

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Queensland Government employees create digital content every day to inform decisions, deliver services, communicate with stakeholders and support business operations.

Good digital content helps:

  • Communicate information clearly
  • Improve decision-making
  • Support collaboration and knowledge sharing
  • Reduce confusion and rework
  • Improve customer and stakeholder experiences
  • Increase accessibility and inclusion
  • Improve productivity and efficiency

Digital content no longer consists solely of documents. Public servants increasingly create and maintain dashboards, online forms, presentations, collaboration spaces, visualisations, videos and AI-assisted content.

Strong content development skills help ensure information remains accurate, accessible, useful and appropriate for the intended audience.

Workplace scenarios

You may be demonstrating this competency when you:

  • Create a briefing note or report in Word
  • Develop a presentation in PowerPoint
  • Build a spreadsheet or dashboard in Excel or Power BI
  • Create a Microsoft Form to collect information
  • Update content on an intranet or SharePoint site
  • Edit an existing document to improve clarity and usability
  • Create a process guide or quick reference resource
  • Prepare visual content such as charts or infographics
  • Draft content with the assistance of Microsoft Copilot
  • Adapt content for different audiences and communication channels
Tools and processes

Tool availability and configuration differ between agencies. Use only systems approved by your agency.

Common tools

Tools are the digital technologies used to create, edit and publish content.

Examples include:

  • Microsoft Word
  • Microsoft PowerPoint
  • Microsoft Excel
  • Microsoft Forms
  • Microsoft OneNote
  • SharePoint
  • Power BI
  • Microsoft Copilot
  • Image, video and design tools approved by your agency
  • Web content management systems

Common processes

Processes are the ways of working that help ensure content remains accurate, useful and fit for purpose.

Examples include:

  • Planning content before creation
  • Identifying audience requirements
  • Applying accessibility principles
  • Using approved templates and branding
  • Reviewing and editing content
  • Incorporating feedback
  • Maintaining version control
  • Applying plain language principles
  • Testing content before publication
  • Updating content when information changes
Try it at work
  • Select an existing document, presentation, form or other piece of content you recently created.
  • Review it from the perspective of its intended audience.
  • Identify whether the content is clear, accurate and easy to understand.
  • Check whether headings, formatting and structure support readability.
  • Consider whether the content is accessible and inclusive.
  • Identify one improvement that would make the content more useful.
  • Implement the improvement.
  • Ask a colleague for feedback on the revised version.

Hint: Good content development is not about creating more content. It is about creating content that helps people understand, decide, act or learn.

3.2 Integrating and re-elaborating digital content

What it is

Modifying, refining, combining and building upon existing digital content to create new content, knowledge or solutions. This includes integrating information from multiple sources, adapting content for different audiences and purposes, and adding value through improvement, analysis or creative reuse.

In practical terms, this means being able to answer:

"How can I use existing content to create something better, more useful or more relevant?"

rather than simply:

"How can I copy and paste this information?"

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Queensland Government employees rarely start with a blank page.

Most work involves bringing together information, data, documents, policies, stakeholder input, research and lessons learned from multiple sources to create new outputs and insights.

Strong content integration skills help employees:

  • Reduce duplication of effort
  • Reuse existing knowledge effectively
  • Create better quality work products
  • Improve consistency across the organisation
  • Save time and resources
  • Produce evidence-based advice and recommendations
  • Translate information for different audiences
  • Generate new insights from existing information

As AI-powered tools become increasingly common, employees also need the ability to critically review, modify and improve content generated by digital technologies rather than simply accepting it as-is.

Workplace scenarios

You may be demonstrating this competency when you:

  • Combining information from multiple reports to prepare a briefing note
  • Creating a presentation from existing documents and data sources
  • Adapting a template for a new purpose
  • Summarising research findings for executive audiences
  • Developing a project plan using content from previous projects
  • Using Microsoft Copilot to draft content and then refining it
  • Combining data from different sources into a dashboard
  • Reusing and improving existing procedures or guidance materials
  • Developing stakeholder communications using content from multiple teams
  • Creating a lessons-learned resource based on previous project experiences
Tools and processes

Tool availability and configuration differ between agencies. Use only systems approved by your agency.

Common tools

Tools are the digital technologies used to combine, modify and improve content.

Examples include:

  • Microsoft Word
  • Microsoft PowerPoint
  • Microsoft Excel
  • Microsoft Copilot
  • SharePoint
  • OneNote
  • Power BI
  • Microsoft Loop
  • Forms and survey platforms
  • Approved design and content creation tools

Common processes

Processes are the ways of working that help ensure integrated content remains accurate, useful and appropriate.

Examples include:

  • Reviewing existing content before creating new content
  • Identifying authoritative sources
  • Synthesising information from multiple sources
  • Adapting content for different audiences
  • Updating and improving existing resources
  • Seeking feedback and incorporating improvements
  • Validating AI-generated outputs
  • Maintaining references and source information
  • Checking consistency across documents and resources
  • Applying accessibility and usability principles
Try it at work
  • Select a current work activity that requires information from multiple sources.
  • Identify at least three existing resources relevant to the task.
  • Review the information for accuracy, relevance and currency.
  • Identify common themes, key messages or insights.
  • Create a short summary, briefing, presentation or visualisation that combines the information.
  • Adapt the content for your intended audience.
  • Ask a colleague to review the final output.
  • Identify how the integrated content provides greater value than the individual sources.

Hint: Integrating content is not about combining everything you can find. It is about selecting, refining and connecting information so that it becomes more useful to the audience.

3.3 Copyright and licences

What it is

Understanding how copyright, licences and related legal and ethical requirements apply to digital content. This includes knowing when content can be used, shared, adapted or published, recognising different licence conditions, attributing sources appropriately and respecting the rights of content creators.

In practical terms, this means being able to answer:

"Am I allowed to use, adapt, share or publish this content?"

rather than simply:

"Can I access or copy this content?"

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Queensland Government employees regularly create, adapt, share and publish digital content.

This may include:

  • Reports
  • Presentations
  • Images
  • Graphics
  • Templates
  • Videos
  • Data
  • Website content
  • Training materials
  • AI-assisted or AI-generated content

Not all digital content can be freely copied, changed or distributed. Copyright applies automatically to many types of original content, and licence conditions determine what others can do with that content. Australian copyright guidance explains that copyright protects original expression in forms such as writing, images, music, films and software code, and gives copyright owners exclusive rights such as copying, publishing and communicating the material.

Strong copyright and licence awareness helps employees:

  • Use digital content lawfully and ethically
  • Reduce legal and reputational risk
  • Respect creators' rights and moral rights
  • Attribute sources appropriately
  • Understand when permission may be required
  • Use Creative Commons and open government material correctly
  • Avoid inappropriate reuse of third-party material
  • Make better decisions when using AI-generated content

Queensland Government generally supports the distribution and reuse of government material, but some content is excluded from Creative Commons licensing, including logos, trademarks, third-party material and material with other specified licence conditions.

Workplace scenarios

You may be demonstrating this competency when you:

  • Use an image in a PowerPoint presentation
  • Reuse content from another agency website or publication
  • Download icons, graphics or templates from the internet
  • Share a report, article or resource with colleagues
  • Publish content on a website, intranet or SharePoint site
  • Adapt an existing document for a new purpose
  • Use open government data in a dashboard or report
  • Apply a Creative Commons licence notice to content
  • Attribute an external source in a briefing, report or learning resource
  • Check whether AI-generated content should be labelled or reviewed before reuse
  • Decide whether permission is required before adapting or publishing content
  • Identify whether Queensland Government branding, logos or third-party content can be reused
Tools and processes

Tool availability and configuration differ between agencies. Use only systems approved by your agency.

Common tools

Tools are the digital systems, platforms or resources used to create, reuse, licence, attribute or publish content.

Examples include:

  • Microsoft Word, PowerPoint and Excel
  • SharePoint and intranet publishing platforms
  • Website content management systems
  • Microsoft 365 stock images, icons and templates
  • Creative Commons licence information
  • Queensland Government website copyright statements
  • Australian Government and Queensland Government open data portals
  • Approved AI tools
  • Agency-approved image, design or media libraries
  • Referencing and citation tools

Common processes

Processes are the checks and decisions that help ensure content is used lawfully, ethically and appropriately.

Examples include:

  • Checking copyright and licence statements before reuse
  • Confirming whether content is government-owned, third-party or open licensed
  • Attributing sources when required
  • Checking whether Creative Commons conditions allow the intended use
  • Seeking permission where no licence or exception applies
  • Reviewing whether content can be adapted, edited or redistributed
  • Labelling AI-generated content where appropriate
  • Keeping records of source material, permissions and licence conditions
  • Checking whether logos, branding or trademarks are excluded from reuse
  • Applying agency approval processes before publishing or distributing content
Try it at work
  • Choose a piece of digital content you want to reuse, such as an image, graphic, table, template, report extract or dataset.
  • Identify where the content came from.
  • Check whether the source includes a copyright or licence statement.
  • Determine whether the content is Queensland Government material, Australian Government material, third-party material, open licensed content or AI-generated content.
  • Check whether the licence allows your intended use, including copying, adapting, sharing or publishing.
  • Identify whether attribution is required.
  • Check whether any exclusions apply, such as logos, branding, trademarks, images or third-party material.
  • Decide whether you can use the content, need to attribute it, need permission or should choose a different source.

Hint: If content is publicly available online, that does not automatically mean it is free to reuse. Always check the copyright and licence conditions before copying, adapting or publishing content.

DigComp 3.0-aligned proficiency guide

DigComp 3.0 describes eight proficiency levels grouped into four broad bands. For simplicity, this Hub presents the four bands: Basic, Intermediate, Advanced and Highly advanced. The descriptions below are practical workplace interpretations of DigComp 3.0 and are not verbatim reproductions of the official competence statements.

Basic

With guidance as needed, individuals can:

  • Recognise general copyright and licence concepts in digital contexts.
  • Recognise that original digital content is automatically protected by copyright.
  • Identify that different types of copyright and licences may apply to digital content, including AI-generated content.
  • Recognise that AI-generated content should be labelled as such where appropriate.
  • Use and share digital content while following basic legal and ethical guidelines.

Intermediate

Individuals can:

  • Recognise that copyright and licensing in digital environments can be complex and should be approached carefully.
  • Distinguish between copyright, trademarks, patents, designs and other intellectual property concepts at a general level.
  • Check licence terms before using, adapting or sharing digital content.
  • Apply Creative Commons or similar open licence conditions correctly.
  • Attribute sources and creators appropriately.
  • Identify when permission may be required before reuse, adaptation or publication.

Advanced

Individuals can:

  • Evaluate copyright and licence conditions across different types of digital content.
  • Apply legal, ethical and organisational requirements when reusing or adapting content.
  • Make informed decisions about using third-party, government, open licensed or AI-generated content.
  • Support others to understand and apply copyright and licence requirements.
  • Identify risks associated with inappropriate reuse, attribution or publication of digital content.

Highly advanced

Individuals can:

  • Develop or improve approaches for managing copyright and licensing in digital content workflows.
  • Guide teams in applying copyright, licence and attribution requirements consistently.
  • Support responsible use of open licences, Creative Commons and government-owned content.
  • Lead improvements in how digital content is sourced, attributed, reused and published.
  • Contribute to organisational practices that support lawful, ethical and transparent content reuse.
Suggested next practice

Copyright and Licence Check

Choose a digital resource you are planning to use in a work product.

Using the downloadable template:

  • Record the content title, source and URL or location.
  • Identify the content type, such as image, document, data, video, template or AI-generated content.
  • Check the copyright or licence statement.
  • Identify what the licence allows.
  • Identify any restrictions, exclusions or attribution requirements.
  • Decide whether the content can be used as-is, adapted, shared or published.
  • Record the attribution statement you would use.
  • Identify whether permission, review or an alternative source is required.

3.4 Computational thinking and programming

What it is

Using logical thinking to break problems into parts, identify patterns, design steps and give instructions to a digital system.

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Not every employee needs to be a programmer, but many roles benefit from understanding how to structure problems, improve workflows and use low-code or automation tools.

This capability supports process improvement, service design, automation, data work and clearer communication with technical teams.

Workplace scenarios

  • Mapping a business process
  • Creating a simple workflow
  • Designing a Microsoft Form
  • Automating a repetitive task
  • Using Excel formulas
  • Building a Power Automate flow
  • Describing requirements for a digital solution
  • Testing whether a digital process works as intended

Tools and processes

  • Process mapping
  • Microsoft Forms
  • Microsoft Excel formulas
  • Power Automate
  • Power Apps
  • Power BI
  • SharePoint lists
  • Workflow diagrams
  • User stories and requirements

Try it at work

Choose one repetitive task and map the process step by step.

Then ask:

  1. What triggers the task?
  2. What information is needed?
  3. What decisions are made?
  4. What steps are repeated?
  5. What could be simplified?
  6. What could be automated?
  7. What risks or approvals need to be considered?

Hint: Before automating a process, check whether the process itself is clear, necessary, approved and well understood.

DigComp 3.0-aligned proficiency guide

Basic

Competence statement: To understand and implement steps to analyse a problem, recognise sub-problems, and plan and develop a sequence of instructions for a computing system to solve a given problem or to perform a specific task.

DigComp proficiency levels: Level 1: with guidance. Level 2: with autonomy and with appropriate guidance when needed.

Intermediate

Competence statement: To understand and implement steps to analyse a problem, recognise sub-problems, and plan and develop a sequence of instructions for a computing system to solve a given problem or to perform a specific task.

DigComp proficiency levels: Level 3: on my own and solving straightforward problems. Level 4: independently, according to my needs, and solving well-defined non-routine problems.

Advanced

Competence statement: To understand and implement steps to analyse a problem, recognise sub-problems, and plan and develop a sequence of instructions for a computing system to solve a given problem or to perform a specific task.

DigComp proficiency levels: Level 5: as well as guiding others. Level 6: adapting to others in complex contexts.

Highly advanced

Competence statement: To understand and implement steps to analyse a problem, recognise sub-problems, and plan and develop a sequence of instructions for a computing system to solve a given problem or to perform a specific task.

DigComp proficiency levels: Level 7: in complex contexts with limited solutions. Level 8: in complex contexts with many interacting factors.

Suggested next practice: Map one common team process and identify one step that could be simplified, standardised or automated.
