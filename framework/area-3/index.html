3. Content creation

Content creation capability helps Queensland Government employees create, edit, improve and manage digital content responsibly. It supports effective communication, service delivery, knowledge sharing, innovation and problem-solving through the purposeful use of digital technologies.

Official DigComp 3.0 area description

To create and edit digital content. To improve and integrate information and content into an existing body of knowledge while understanding how copyright and licences are to be applied, adopting an ethical and responsible approach in the creation, improvement and integration of digital content. To know how to apply computational thinking and programming techniques to give instructions to a computer system.

Why this area matters

Queensland Government employees create digital content every day, including briefs, reports, procedures, presentations, web content, records, dashboards, forms, guidance material, data products and communication resources.

Strong capability in this area helps employees create clear, accessible, accurate and responsible content. It also supports better information sharing, improved service delivery and more effective use of digital tools, including approved AI-enabled tools.

Using workplace tools

Tool availability and approval varies across agencies.

Employees should only use approved content creation, collaboration, automation and AI-enabled tools in accordance with agency requirements, information security, privacy, copyright, accessibility and recordkeeping obligations.

3.1 Developing digital content

What it is

Creating and editing digital content in different formats. This includes developing documents, presentations, spreadsheets, forms, images, videos and other digital resources, as well as improving and adapting existing content for different audiences and purposes.

In practical terms, this means being able to answer:

"How can I create digital content that is useful, clear and fit for purpose?"

rather than simply:

"How do I make this document?"

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Queensland Government employees create digital content every day to inform decisions, deliver services, communicate with stakeholders and support business operations.

Good digital content helps:

  • Communicate information clearly
  • Improve decision-making
  • Support collaboration and knowledge sharing
  • Reduce confusion and rework
  • Improve customer and stakeholder experiences
  • Increase accessibility and inclusion
  • Improve productivity and efficiency

Digital content no longer consists solely of documents. Public servants increasingly create and maintain dashboards, online forms, presentations, collaboration spaces, visualisations, videos and AI-assisted content.

Strong content development skills help ensure information remains accurate, accessible, useful and appropriate for the intended audience.

Workplace scenarios

You may be demonstrating this competency when you:

  • Create a briefing note or report in Word
  • Develop a presentation in PowerPoint
  • Build a spreadsheet or dashboard in Excel or Power BI
  • Create a Microsoft Form to collect information
  • Update content on an intranet or SharePoint site
  • Edit an existing document to improve clarity and usability
  • Create a process guide or quick reference resource
  • Prepare visual content such as charts or infographics
  • Draft content with the assistance of Microsoft Copilot
  • Adapt content for different audiences and communication channels
Tools and processes

Tool availability and configuration differ between agencies. Use only systems approved by your agency.

Common tools

Tools are the digital technologies used to create, edit and publish content.

Examples include:

  • Microsoft Word
  • Microsoft PowerPoint
  • Microsoft Excel
  • Microsoft Forms
  • Microsoft OneNote
  • SharePoint
  • Power BI
  • Microsoft Copilot
  • Image, video and design tools approved by your agency
  • Web content management systems

Common processes

Processes are the ways of working that help ensure content remains accurate, useful and fit for purpose.

Examples include:

  • Planning content before creation
  • Identifying audience requirements
  • Applying accessibility principles
  • Using approved templates and branding
  • Reviewing and editing content
  • Incorporating feedback
  • Maintaining version control
  • Applying plain language principles
  • Testing content before publication
  • Updating content when information changes
Apply this competency at work
  1. Select an existing document, presentation, form or other piece of content you recently created.
  2. Review it from the perspective of its intended audience.
  3. Identify whether the content is clear, accurate and easy to understand.
  4. Check whether headings, formatting and structure support readability.
  5. Consider whether the content is accessible and inclusive.
  6. Identify one improvement that would make the content more useful.
  7. Implement the improvement.
  8. Ask a colleague for feedback on the revised version.

Hint: Good content development is not about creating more content. It is about creating content that helps people understand, decide, act or learn.

DigComp 3.0-aligned proficiency guide

DigComp 3.0 describes eight proficiency levels grouped into four broad bands. For simplicity, this Hub presents the four bands: Basic, Intermediate, Advanced and Highly advanced. The descriptions below are practical workplace interpretations of DigComp 3.0 and are not verbatim reproductions of the official competence statements.

Basic

With guidance as needed, individuals can:

  • Create simple digital content using common tools.
  • Edit or update existing digital content.
  • Understand that different types of content serve different purposes.
  • Recognise basic principles of accessible and effective content creation.

Intermediate

Individuals can:

  • Create digital content in a variety of formats.
  • Adapt content for different audiences and contexts.
  • Combine information from multiple sources to develop content.
  • Use digital tools confidently to create and edit content.
  • Improve existing content to make it more useful and effective.

Advanced

Individuals can:

  • Develop high-quality content for complex needs and audiences.
  • Select the most appropriate format for different communication purposes.
  • Support others in developing digital content.
  • Apply accessibility, usability and design principles.
  • Improve content creation processes and outcomes.

Highly advanced

Individuals can:

  • Design and lead complex digital content initiatives.
  • Develop new approaches to creating and managing content.
  • Guide others in advanced content development practices.
  • Evaluate and improve organisational approaches to content creation.
  • Support innovation in digital content development.

3.2 Integrating and re-elaborating digital content

What it is

Modifying, refining, combining and adapting existing information or content to create something new and useful.

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Government work often requires employees to bring together information from multiple sources to create new advice, guidance, summaries, reports, presentations or service content.

Strong capability in this competence helps employees synthesise information responsibly, avoid duplication and create content that is accurate, useful and fit for purpose.

Workplace scenarios

  • Combining research findings into a briefing note
  • Summarising consultation feedback
  • Turning policy information into plain language guidance
  • Creating a presentation from a report
  • Reworking existing content for a different audience
  • Combining data, text and visuals into a dashboard or update
  • Using Copilot or another approved AI tool to summarise or reframe content, with human review

Tools and processes

  • Microsoft Word
  • Microsoft PowerPoint
  • Microsoft Excel
  • Microsoft SharePoint
  • Microsoft OneNote
  • Power BI
  • Copilot or approved AI-assisted tools
  • Agency templates and style guidance

Apply this competency at work

Choose three existing sources related to a current task and create a short synthesis for a specific audience.

Check:

  1. What information is essential?
  2. What can be removed?
  3. What needs to be rewritten for clarity?
  4. What sources need to be acknowledged?
  5. What needs to be checked before sharing?
  6. What format will be most useful?

Hint: When reusing or adapting existing content, check whether the original content is current, approved for reuse and appropriate for the new audience or purpose.

DigComp 3.0-aligned proficiency guide

Basic

Competence statement: To modify, refine and integrate new information and content into existing knowledge and resources to create new and original content and knowledge.

DigComp proficiency levels: Level 1: with guidance. Level 2: with autonomy and with appropriate guidance when needed.

Intermediate

Competence statement: To modify, refine and integrate new information and content into existing knowledge and resources to create new and original content and knowledge.

DigComp proficiency levels: Level 3: on my own and solving straightforward problems. Level 4: independently, according to my needs, and solving well-defined non-routine problems.

Advanced

Competence statement: To modify, refine and integrate new information and content into existing knowledge and resources to create new and original content and knowledge.

DigComp proficiency levels: Level 5: as well as guiding others. Level 6: adapting to others in complex contexts.

Highly advanced

Competence statement: To modify, refine and integrate new information and content into existing knowledge and resources to create new and original content and knowledge.

DigComp proficiency levels: Level 7: in complex contexts with limited solutions. Level 8: in complex contexts with many interacting factors.

Suggested next practice: Take a long document and turn it into a one-page summary for a specific audience. Check whether the key message is clear without needing to read the original document.

3.3 Copyright and licences

What it is

Understanding how copyright, licences, permissions, attribution and related ethical obligations apply to digital content.

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Government employees regularly use, create, publish and share content. This may include text, images, templates, data, diagrams, reports, learning resources and third-party material.

Understanding copyright and licensing helps reduce legal, reputational and compliance risks and supports responsible reuse of information.

Workplace scenarios

  • Using an image in a presentation
  • Reusing text from an external source
  • Publishing content on a public website
  • Sharing templates or learning materials
  • Using Creative Commons licensed material
  • Reusing data or charts from another source
  • Checking whether AI-generated content can be used

Tools and processes

  • Copyright notices
  • Creative Commons licences
  • Agency publishing guidance
  • Source tracking
  • Attribution statements
  • Information management processes
  • Approved image and media libraries

Apply this competency at work

Review one document, presentation or web page and identify:

  1. Which content was created internally?
  2. Which content came from another source?
  3. Which images, charts or diagrams require attribution?
  4. Whether external material is approved for reuse?
  5. Whether copyright or licensing information is clear?
  6. Whether any content needs to be removed, replaced or acknowledged?

Hint: Do not assume that content found online is free to reuse. Check the licence, source, copyright status and agency requirements before using or publishing material.

Competence statement: To understand how copyright and licences, as well as associated legal and ethical issues, apply to digital content, and how to correctly apply them.

Intermediate

Suggested next practice: Create a simple attribution checklist for your team to use when adding images, diagrams, charts or external content to documents and presentations.

3.4 Computational thinking and programming

What it is

Using logical thinking to break problems into parts, identify patterns, design steps and give instructions to a digital system.

Why it matters in Queensland Government

Not every employee needs to be a programmer, but many roles benefit from understanding how to structure problems, improve workflows and use low-code or automation tools.

This capability supports process improvement, service design, automation, data work and clearer communication with technical teams.

Workplace scenarios

  • Mapping a business process
  • Creating a simple workflow
  • Designing a Microsoft Form
  • Automating a repetitive task
  • Using Excel formulas
  • Building a Power Automate flow
  • Describing requirements for a digital solution
  • Testing whether a digital process works as intended

Tools and processes

  • Process mapping
  • Microsoft Forms
  • Microsoft Excel formulas
  • Power Automate
  • Power Apps
  • Power BI
  • SharePoint lists
  • Workflow diagrams
  • User stories and requirements

Apply this competency at work

Choose one repetitive task and map the process step by step.

Then ask:

  1. What triggers the task?
  2. What information is needed?
  3. What decisions are made?
  4. What steps are repeated?
  5. What could be simplified?
  6. What could be automated?
  7. What risks or approvals need to be considered?

Hint: Before automating a process, check whether the process itself is clear, necessary, approved and well understood.
